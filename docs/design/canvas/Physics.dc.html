THE PHYSICS ESCALATES WITH THE ARGUMENT
ROUND 1 · DEBATE GESTURE ONLY
Nobody touches anybody. Pointing, arms folded, exasperated shrugs. Contact would be wrong here — it is still an argument.
ROUND 2 · ROAST SHOVE
Contact begins, but it is shoving and recoiling — no launches. The escalation has to be felt, not announced.
ROUND 3 · FIGHT FULL LAUNCH
Everything above is unlocked. The physics arriving late is what makes Round 3 land as the climax rather than more of the same.
DERIVED, NOT SIMULATED
Launch distance, spin and hang time are a pure function of damage and the match seed — no live solver. Nothing extra goes on the wire, both screens agree, and the clip replays identically.
LIMBS LAG THE TORSO
Arms and legs trail two or three frames behind the body they are attached to. It is the cheapest possible trick and it is most of what separates weight from a sprite being slid across the screen.
YOUR TYPOS TRIP YOU
Self-damage already exists in the rules — physics is what makes it funny. An uncorrected error makes your own figure stumble over its feet mid-sentence. The joke lands without a single new mechanic.
KO GOES FULL RAGDOLL
Exactly once per match, at the KO, everything goes limp and flops. Reserved for the last frame so it stays an event — and it is the frame the clip opens on.
The one hard limit: all of this lives in the stage band above the typing panel, which is opaque and painted over it. A figure can be launched the full width of the screen, bounce off the wall and slide down it without moving one character the player is reading. When a physics idea cannot obey that, the physics loses — not the text.
